# Open Origins: research plan

**Aim for 2027:** R-open. That means a pathway map covering at least 4 scenarios with every step scored, at least 10 published claims benched against nulls, and at least 3 hashed predictions confirmed by later data. R-full is out of reach (under 1%), but the map shows exactly which step blocks it.

The work runs in waves. The lead reviews each wave: what to drop, what to escalate, what to try next. Fable is used sparingly, at most once per night, for a second opinion on the overall direction. Its one review is saved as `docs/fable_review_N.md`.

## The question tree

1. **Can any route be continuous?** Which hand-offs between steps break under a compatibility rule fixed in advance? That decides where the field's real gap is.
2. **Which headline claims survive proper nulls?** Autocatalytic sets in metabolism, assembly index as a biosignature, LUCA's lifestyle, "functional RNAs are common".
3. **Can we make predictions that future data will judge?** Bennu and Ryugu compounds, QT45 mutants, new ribozyme scans.
4. **How close is L3, sustained replication?** QT45's real numbers.

## Order and risk

B1 and B2 come first, because an invented citation or a rule bent after the fact would discredit everything later. B5 → B6 and B7 are cheap, need no wet lab, and are decisive either way, so they run in wave 1. B11 and B12 are where confirmation can come from. Seal them early, because the clock only starts when the hash is written.

## What each morning review contains

- what landed, with numbers and paths;
- which claims are benched, and which are closed (added to the negative atlas);
- which citations were verified, corrected or struck;
- the lead's review and the next wave;
- decisions only the human lead can make, such as asking a lab to run a sealed test.
