## Short answer

1. **The lab windows are established.** Pure fatty-acid vesicles fail at about 0.5–2 mM Mg²⁺. Mixtures with a monoglyceride or an alcohol hold to about 4–10 mM. Most RNA copying is run at 50 mM or more. This is in Chen 2005, Adamala 2013 and later papers. Nothing is left to test there.
2. **"Hot-spring water lets vesicles form, seawater does not" is established**, for a handful of waters. Milshteyn 2018 (two Yellowstone pools and seawater), Joshi 2017 (three Ladakh springs), Joshi 2021 (two New Zealand pools).
3. **The closest work to T4 is Cary et al. 2024.** They measured the cation level at which vesicles flocculate and set it beside a short list of hot-spring analyses taken from other papers. It covers the vesicle side only, about eight named springs, and no RNA condition.
4. **Cohen et al. 2024 did the joint test for two soda lakes**, in the lab, with real lake water. They then say it is "unclear" whether hot springs or vents can do the same.
5. **The exact T4 design was not found.** I found no paper that runs a vesicle window and an RNA window jointly over a database of measured natural waters.
6. **But the blind count shows the answer follows from two margins** (see the prereg). So the gap is real, and the study that fills it is a count, not a kill test.

## Closest prior art, in detail

**Cary et al. 2024 (opened).** This is the nearest thing to the vesicle half of T4.

- Their measurement: "Mass flocculation of lipid vesicles occurs beyond ~1.9 mM of Ca, Fe, and Mg cations in this study, while natural settings on Earth are observed to be <0.7 mM".
- Their field numbers: "The concentrations of cations in natural hot-spring water on Earth are 0.01–0.45 mM for Ca 2+ , 0.001–0.12 mM for Mg 2+ , and 0.0004–0.6123 mM for Fe 2+".
- Their field list: "Midway Geyser Basin, Norris Geyser Basin, Bison Pool, and Chocolate Pots in Yellowstone", two New Zealand sites and Kamchatka, taken from Deamer 2019 and Wu 2013.
- What it is not: a database, a joint test, or a test with an RNA condition.
- A tension inside the same group's papers: Milshteyn 2018, Table 2, gives Mammoth Hot Springs at Ca "305 ± 60 ppm", "7.6 mM". That is ten times the "<0.7 mM" above.

**Cohen et al. 2024 (opened).** This is the nearest thing to the joint half.

- Water from two Canadian soda lakes, "∼1 M Na + and ∼1 mM Mg 2+ near pH 10".
- Primer extension "at comparable rates in lake water and standard laboratory conditions (50 mM MgCl 2 , pH 8)".
- A ligase ribozyme worked after the pH was lowered "from ∼10 to 9".
- Vesicles of 1:1 decanoic acid and decanol kept their dye in lake water, but only if they were formed first in dilute water.
- The claim T4 would test: "Fatty acid vesicles have been shown to form in other putative early Earth environments, such as subaerial hot springs ( 52 , 53 ) and deep-sea hydrothermal vents ( 54 ); however, it is unclear whether any of these environments can also support the assembly of RNA, suggesting a possible unique role for soda lakes during the origin of life."

**Milshteyn et al. 2018 (opened).**

- Two Yellowstone waters (pH 3.3 and pH 7.9) and Santa Cruz seawater.
- "the dilute ionic solutes of hydrothermal water are not a barrier to vesicle assembly in analogue prebiotic conditions".
- "none of the amphiphilic compounds formed membranes in seawater".
- Lauric acid with its monoglyceride formed membranes at both pH values. This matters for the pH limits below.

**Jordan et al. 2019 (opened as the accepted manuscript).**

- "Warm (70 °C), alkaline (pH 12) solutions equivalent to those found in modern alkaline hydrothermal vents actively promote vesicle formation and mitigate the interference produced by high concentrations of NaCl, Mg2+, and Ca2+."
- No field water was used. No RNA condition was tested.

**Damer & Deamer 2020 (opened).**

- "the much higher concentrations of ionic solutes composing seawater inhibit self-assembly of membranous structures and encapsulation of polymers. This is not a problem for self-assembly of membranes in hot spring water supplied by precipitation with its much lower concentrations of ionic solutes (Milshteyn et al. , 2018 )."
- "the initial modest concentrations of the various ionic solutes of hydrothermal fields increase a 100-fold or more as most of the water is lost to the atmosphere."

**Joshi et al. 2017 (opened).** A caution for any window drawn from buffer experiments.

- In three real hot-spring waters, only the acid plus monoglyceride mixtures formed vesicles. "The remaining systems, however, formed vesicles only in bicine buffer."
- Oleic acid alone, and oleic acid with oleyl alcohol, "did not form vesicles in the hot spring waters, although they could form vesicles in bicine buffer".
- So a water can sit inside every lab limit and still not give vesicles. A window count is an upper bound.

So the RNA side has two honest readings.

- **R1, Mg ≥ 1 mM.** The lowest level at which an opened source shows copying or ribozyme activity.
- **R50, Mg ≥ 50 mM.** The level the field treats as standard.

Choices that are mine, not a source's:

- Ca for pure acids has no direct number in an opened source. I set it equal to the Mg limit, because Cary rank Ca as the stronger flocculant.
- "pH about 12" is coded as pH ≥ 11.5.
- The pH limits are the weakest part. Milshteyn saw membranes at pH 3.3 in real spring water. So every pH-dependent result is checked with the pH limits removed.

## What is established, and what is not

| Question | Status |
|---|---|
| Do divalent cations break fatty-acid vesicles at low mM? | Established (Chen 2005 and others) |
| Do mixtures widen the window? | Established (Chen 2005; Mansy 2008; Jordan 2019; Adamala 2016) |
| Does standard RNA copying need more Mg than vesicles can take? | Established, and true by the numbers alone: 50 mM against 0.5–10 mM |
| Do vesicles form in a few real hot-spring waters and not in seawater? | Established (Milshteyn 2018; Joshi 2017, 2021) |
| Are hot-spring cations below the flocculation level, for a list of named springs? | Done (Cary 2024) |
| Do two real soda lakes support RNA copying, a ribozyme and vesicles at about 1 mM Mg? | Done (Cohen 2024) |
| Vesicle and RNA windows run jointly over a database of natural waters | Not found |
| Vent-fluid databases checked against the alkaline-mixture window of Jordan 2019 | Not found |
